# Duskline — Reviews Section Redesign

## The Layout Problem

Four-to-eight cards, all visible at once, all 5 stars, all similarly-polished trade jargon — reads like copy, not customer feedback. Real reviews are uneven: different lengths, mixed ratings, some boring, one with a minor complaint buried in an otherwise positive note.

## Recommended Layout — Amazon-Style Summary + Expand

**Top of section:**
- Aggregate score: **4.7 out of 5** (based on 23 reviews) — a number that isn't a round 5.0
- Simple horizontal bar breakdown: 5★ (70%), 4★ (22%), 3★ (6%), 2★ (2%), 1★ (0%)
- One featured review shown in full below the summary (pick the most specific, least salesy one — see "Sealed connectors" below)
- **"Show all 23 reviews"** button/accordion — collapsed by default, expands into a simple stacked list (not a grid of cards), more like a scrollable feed than a wall of tiles

**Why this is better than the current grid:**
- A single visible review + a number does the credibility work; the person doesn't need to read eight cards to believe it
- An expandable list feels like something with actual volume behind it, rather than "here are all four testimonials we have"
- Removes the pressure to make every visible card equally polished — buried reviews further down the list can be shorter, blander, even slightly negative, without hurting the first impression

## De-identification — Simplify the Badge

Drop the eye-slash icon and "Names hidden by request" framing — it draws attention to the concealment rather than just doing it quietly. Use a plain, boring badge instead, the way Amazon/Google reviews do it:

> **Electrician · SA** · Verified trade order

No icon, no explanation needed. "Verified trade order" (or "Verified purchase" for consumer-side reviews) does the trust work without narrating why the name isn't shown.

## Notes
- The 3-star and two 4-stars aren't there to be negative for its own sake — they're there because a page with zero friction anywhere reads as curated (because it is). One honest gripe about lead time is more convincing than another glowing 5-star.
- Once these placeholders are replaced with real customer feedback, keep this same principle: don't smooth every review to sound the same, and don't only publish the ones that are uniformly positive — a visible 3 or 4 star with a fair complaint is doing more trust-building work than another perfect one.
- Worth a reminder here since it applies once this goes live: these need to be real reviews from real customers once launched, not written-to-sound-real placeholders passed off as genuine — publishing fabricated testimonials is a misleading-advertising problem under the Australian Consumer Law, not just a style risk.
