## Positioning note before the copy

This isn't a different product — it's the same factory-sealed, plug-and-play system you already sell, packaged into fixed preset lengths and sold direct rather than through the quote pipeline. No electrician required because it plugs into an existing GPO, same as every current system does — not because anything about the seal is different. Keep that distinction explicit in the copy: DIY doesn't mean lower-grade, it means simpler to buy and easier to install yourself.

### 3. How it works — browse kits, pick a length, join the list

This is a **kit gallery**, not a linear three-step form. The person flicks through a few application-based kits, sees the run-length options for each, and can join the waitlist right from the kit they're looking at — no separate page, no forced sequence.

**Section header:** Find the kit that suits your space.

**Kit cards (carousel or grid — confirm which suits the build), one per application:**

| Kit | Application | Channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stair Kit** | Step edges, stair nosing | Aluminium | Preset lengths matched to typical stair runs |
| **Pool Surround Kit** | Pool coping, pond edges | **Stainless only** | No aluminium option offered here — decided for the customer, not a choice they have to make |
| **Patio Kit** | Deck edges, patio perimeter | Aluminium | Longer preset lengths available |
| **Path Kit** | Garden paths, walkways | Aluminium | Shorter, curve-friendly presets |

*(Confirm the final kit list and whether stairs/patio/path also get a stainless upsell option, or stay aluminium-only by design — "pools only get stainless" reads as a deliberate simplification, worth deciding if any other kit gets the same locked-in treatment rather than a choice.)*

**Each kit card shows, without needing a click-through:**

- The available preset lengths for that kit (e.g. 3m / 5m / 8m — confirm actual presets per kit, ideally tied to the 10m single-feed limit so no CC-strip complexity enters this tier)

- **A waitlist button right on the card** — join the list for *that* kit specifically, not just the page generally

**Clicking into a kit** opens a fuller view (still same page, not a new URL) showing:
- What's included for that specific kit
- The control options available (see below)
- The same waitlist form, pre-filled with which kit they were looking at

### 4. Control options

**Section header:** Set it and forget it.

- **Fixed-on** — plug in, it's on. Simplest option.
- **Wall dimmer** — manual brightness control, no app required.
- **Smart app control** — WiFi-connected, adjust from your phone, works with Google Home and Alexa. Includes **automatic dusk-to-dawn scheduling** — set it once and it turns on and off with the sun.

⚠️ **Do not publish the smart control claims until confirmed sourced.** This is the same category of controller flagged on an earlier quote (Tuya-ecosystem, Google Home/Alexa compatible) but it was swapped out at the time because it wasn't in the confirmed control catalogue — nothing's been costed or locked in with Ava yet. "GPS-based" or "comes home and turns on" style claims belong to the customer's own phone automation (a Google Home/Alexa routine triggered by location), not something the driver itself does — word this as "works with your existing smart home routines" rather than claiming Orenara supplies location-based automation directly.

## Build notes for Replit (once copy is approved)

- New page, new route (e.g. `/diy-kit` or `/kits`)
- **Kit gallery is one page, not separate URLs per kit** — clicking into a kit expands/reveals detail in place (accordion, modal, or expanding card — Replit's call on the cleanest implementation), so the waitlist form can carry "which kit" context without a page reload
- **Two distinct tracked events needed, not one:**
  1. **Kit view event** — fires when someone opens/expands a specific kit (Stair / Pool Surround / Patio / Path). This is the "what people are actually interested in before they even commit to a form" signal — arguably more valuable than the form submit alone, since it captures browsing intent from people who don't convert too.
  2. **Waitlist submit event** — fires on form submission, same as the original conversion plan, but should carry the kit name as a parameter (e.g. GA4 event parameter `kit_name`) so submits can be broken down by kit later, not just counted as one blended total.
- New form → same Resend pipeline pattern as the existing enquiry form
- No payment processing, no cart — this is registration-only
- Standard checkpoint/approval discipline applies: brief in phases, no push to GitHub without explicit approval, verify build passes before deploy, spot-check the public page after
- Once live and both events are confirmed firing, the Google Ads conversion actions can be created via the Windsor.ai connector already in place — likely two: "DIY Kit Interest" (view-based, higher volume, softer signal) and "DIY Kit Waitlist" (submit-based, harder signal), so Google Ads can eventually optimise toward the real conversion rather than just view volume
